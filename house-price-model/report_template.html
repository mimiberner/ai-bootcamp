What the KPIs mean

RMSE
Root mean squared error. The typical size of a miss, with big misses penalised heavily. The main ranking metric.
MAE
Mean absolute error. The average miss in dollars. Less sensitive to a few large errors than RMSE.
Median error
Half of all predictions miss by less than this. The most intuitive “typical miss”.
MAPE
Mean absolute percentage error. The average miss as a share of the true price.
Within ±10%
Share of districts where the prediction landed within 10% of the true price.
R²
Share of the variation in prices the model explains. 1.0 is perfect; 0 means no better than guessing the average.
Overfit gap
Train R² minus test R². A large gap means the model memorised the training data and generalises less well.
CV RMSE
RMSE averaged over 5-fold cross-validation on the training set, ± one standard deviation. A check that the test result isn't luck.
Training time
Seconds to fit on 16,512 rows using 4 CPU cores.

How this was run

  • Data.
  • Features. Location (latitude, longitude, distance to ocean), housing age, rooms, bedrooms, population, households and median income, plus three ratios: rooms per household, bedrooms per room and people per household. Missing bedroom counts (1%) are filled with the median.
  • Target. Every model predicts the log of the price, then converts back. This makes errors relative, so a $50K miss on a $100K home counts more than on a $450K home. Predictions are clipped to the $15K–$500K range found in the data.
  • Scaling. Linear regression and the neural network get standardised, log-compressed inputs. Without that, a handful of extreme districts produced predictions above $1.5M. Tree models don't need it.
  • Model sizes. Kept deliberately small so the whole benchmark trains in under a minute. The boosted models would likely gain another 2–4% with tuning.
  • Why LightGBM as the extra method. Gradient-boosted trees are usually the strongest choice for tabular data like this, and LightGBM is fast. The stacked ensemble tests whether blending the three tree models helps further.
